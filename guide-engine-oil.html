How to Choose the Right Engine Oil in India: Grade, Specification and Change Interval

Engine oil should be selected from the vehicle manufacturer's recommended specifications first. Viscosity, performance standards, engine type, climate and service interval matter more than marketing claims on the bottle.

Quick takeaway: Choose the product based on your actual use and the manufacturer's specifications where applicable. Compare total ownership value, not just the headline price.

1. Start with the owner's manual

The manufacturer's recommended viscosity and specification are the first things to check. Do not replace a required specification with a different grade simply because it is more expensive.

2. Understand viscosity grades

Numbers such as 5W-30 or 10W-40 describe how the oil behaves across temperature ranges. The correct grade depends on the engine and the manufacturer's recommendation.

3. Check performance specifications

Look for the applicable API, ACEA, JASO or manufacturer approval specified for your vehicle. The exact requirement varies by engine and application.

4. Choose the oil type

Mineral, semi-synthetic and fully synthetic oils differ in formulation and performance characteristics. The correct specification matters more than choosing the most premium category.

5. Match oil to the engine type

Petrol, diesel, turbocharged, motorcycle and other engines can have different requirements. For motorcycles, check whether the oil is designed for the specific clutch and gearbox arrangement where applicable.

6. Buy the correct quantity

Use the vehicle manual or service information for the required oil quantity. Overfilling can be harmful, so do not assume that using the entire bottle is correct.

7. Don't extend intervals blindly

Follow the manufacturer's service interval and adjust only when the manufacturer specifies different schedules for severe use. Traffic, dust, short trips and heavy loads can affect service conditions.

8. Check authenticity and packaging

Buy from reputable sellers and inspect seals, labels, batch information and packaging. Be cautious of unusually cheap products from uncertain sources.

9. Oil filter matters too

When the service schedule calls for an oil-filter replacement, changing only the oil may not be the appropriate service. Follow the vehicle manufacturer's maintenance schedule.

10. Common mistakes

Do not choose oil purely by brand, colour or price. Do not mix products casually when the required specification is unknown, and do not ignore the exact viscosity recommendation.

11. Bottom line

For engine oil, 'correct specification' beats 'most expensive'. Use the manufacturer's required grade and standard, buy from a trustworthy source and follow the recommended service interval.
