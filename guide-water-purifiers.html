How to Choose a Water Purifier in India: RO, UV, UF and What Your Water Needs

There is no single purification technology that is best for every Indian household. The right choice depends on the source and quality of your water, especially its dissolved solids and contamination risk, plus electricity, storage and maintenance requirements.

Quick takeaway: Choose the product based on your actual use and the manufacturer's specifications where applicable. Compare total ownership value, not just the headline price.

1. Know your water source

Municipal supply, borewell water, tanker water and mixed sources can have different characteristics. Test the water or obtain reliable local information before choosing a purification system.

2. Understand RO

Reverse osmosis can reduce dissolved salts and many other impurities, making it useful for water with high dissolved solids when the system is appropriately selected and maintained.

3. Understand UV and UF

UV is designed to inactivate microorganisms, while UF uses a membrane to physically separate certain particles and microorganisms. Their suitability depends on the water characteristics and the manufacturer's design.

4. Don't choose RO from marketing alone

RO is not automatically necessary for every water source. It can also reject some water during operation and requires periodic filter and membrane maintenance.

5. Check TDS carefully

TDS is a useful indicator of dissolved substances, but it is not a complete water-safety test. Do not use a TDS number alone to determine whether water is safe or which purifier is required.

6. Storage capacity

If your area has interruptions, a storage tank can be useful. Choose capacity based on household consumption rather than buying the largest tank available.

7. Electricity requirement

Some systems require electricity for pumps or UV treatment. Consider whether the purifier will remain usable during power cuts and whether manual storage is important.

8. Filter replacement cost

The purchase price is only part of ownership cost. Check the expected replacement schedule and price of filters, membranes and other consumables.

9. Installation and water pressure

Confirm inlet pressure, drainage and installation requirements. Some RO systems require a drain connection and sufficient pressure for proper operation.

10. Service availability

A purifier is only as useful as its maintenance. Check whether authorised service and replacement filters are realistically available in your area.

11. Common mistakes

Avoid choosing solely from the number of purification stages, UV/RO labels or a discount. Do not assume more stages automatically mean safer or better water.

12. Bottom line

First understand the water, then select the appropriate technology. Compare purification capability, wastage, storage, electricity, maintenance and service cost before buying.
